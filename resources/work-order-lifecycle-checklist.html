Work Order Lifecycle Checklist

From assignment to payment, seven steps. Run this checklist on every order before you submit.

The seven steps

1. Assignment

Read the work order line by line. Note the property, the task, the deadline, and the allowable. Confirm the coverage area is yours.

2. Field visit

Perform the task and capture photos: before, during, and after, plus every view the work order lists. Camera date and timestamps on.

3. Documentation

Label and organize the photos, complete the property condition report, write clear notes. No readable addresses, names, or order numbers left in frame.

4. Processing

Complete the work order in the portal: line items entered, bids attached, invoice prepared. Match every amount to the allowable or the approved bid.

5. Quality control

Check everything against the client's requirements before submission: photos present and in order, dates correct, amounts matched, notes clear.

6. Submission and review

Submit. If it comes back as a reopen, answer it the same day: a clear note explaining what happened, plus the photos that prove it.

7. Payment

Track the invoice to payment per your terms. Log any backcharge, and fix the cause so it does not happen twice.

The five mistakes that cause reopens

  • Vague bid scopes. "Fix fence" tells a reviewer nothing. Say what, where, and how much.
  • Missing photo sequences. No before, or no after. The order is reopened, payment waits, and someone drives back to reshoot.
  • Late submissions. Deadlines are firm. A late order risks a deduction or losing the work.
  • Work above the allowable without an approved bid. You usually absorb the difference.
  • Weak reopen documentation. A reopen answered with no clear note or photos leaves the backcharge in place.

None of these are field-skill problems. They are paperwork problems, and they are fixable. Check for all five before anything is submitted, so a gap surfaces while the crew is still on site, not after a deduction lands.
