Photo Documentation Checklist

The standard that keeps work orders from coming back. Keep this on the truck.

"Before, during, and after, for every task. One task, three views."

If you cut grass, show the lot before, the work, and the finished lot

On every work order

Before photos

Show the condition exactly as you found it, wide shots first, then close-ups of each problem area.

During photos

Show the work actually happening. A worker, the tool, the task in progress. This is what proves the work was done, not just the result.

After photos

Show the finished result, same angles as the before shots where possible, so a reviewer can compare them side by side.

Every listed view

Read the work order line by line. If it asks for an address photo, a street sign, a roof shot, a utility meter, take it. A missing listed photo is one of the easiest reopens to avoid.

Property overview set

Front, both sides, and back of the property, plus the address or mailbox view. Most portals expect these on inspection and maintenance orders.

Before you leave the property

Datestamps on and correct

Portals check dates. A photo with no date, or the wrong date, can be questioned even when the work was done correctly. Check your camera date before the first shot of the day.

Nothing readable that shouldn't be

No addresses, license plates, borrower information, or personal items in frame. Blur or retake anything that could identify a person or a specific unit.

Photos labeled and in order

Name or tag them before you leave the driveway: before, during, after, then by room or area. Labeling later is how photos get lost.

Recurring work

Seasonal reuse, where portal rules allow

For recurring grass cuts, photos can sometimes be reused properly across a season. Ask your processor how it works for each portal, so you do not reshoot what you do not have to.

A few extra photos cost you seconds. A reopen costs you a return trip.
